# Case study

A case study shows how you think. Specific and truthful beats polished and vague.

1. **Gather facts.** Read the repo, commit history (`git log --oneline | head -50`), README and any analytics or notes the user can share. Ask the user (ask_user) for numbers and the client's goal if you cannot find them.
2. **Structure** (aim for 600 to 900 words):
   - **Summary.** Two sentences: who it was for, what you made, the result.
   - **Problem.** What was wrong or missing, for whom, and what it cost them.
   - **Constraints.** Budget, time, tech, team size, content you did not control.
   - **Process.** What you did in order: research, options considered, prototypes, build, testing. Short paragraphs, not a diary.
   - **Key decisions.** Three to five. For each: the options, what you chose, and why. Include one thing you got wrong and changed.
   - **Outcome.** Numbers with a time frame and a baseline ("Booking requests went from 4 to 11 a week over six weeks"). If there are no numbers yet, say so and describe what you will measure.
   - **What I would do next.** One or two lines.
3. **Honesty rules.** Never invent metrics, testimonials or logos. Say "I" when it was you and "we" only for real teams. Do not claim credit for the client's results unless the link is clear. Round honestly.
4. **Visuals list.** End with a checklist of images to capture: before and after screenshots, one process sketch or diagram, the key screen at mobile and desktop, a short screen recording, a chart of the main result. Give each a file name and a caption.
5. **Voice.** Plain sentences, past tense, no buzzwords. Read it aloud once.

Save as `case-study.md`. Offer a 60-word version for a portfolio card.
