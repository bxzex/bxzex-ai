# Web research

Reading is slow for you, so be deliberate.

1. Write the exact question in one line. Decide what a good answer looks like before searching.
2. Search with the words a good source would use, not the user's wording. Add a year for anything that changes.
3. Read the snippets first. They often contain the answer. Only fetch a page when the snippets disagree or lack the detail.
4. Fetch at most two or three pages. Prefer the primary source: the official docs, the company's own page, the paper, the government site. Skip content farms and forum guesses.
5. If two sources disagree, say so and say which you trust and why.
6. Check the date on anything about prices, versions, laws or people's jobs.

When you answer:
- Lead with the answer, then how you know.
- Name the source and give its URL for each key fact.
- Say plainly what you could not confirm. Never fill a gap with a guess dressed as a fact.
- Do not invent quotes, numbers or links.
