# User research

1. **Write the goal.** One sentence: "We want to learn how X people do Y today and where it hurts." List 3 to 5 questions you need answered. If you cannot name a decision the answers will change, stop and rethink.
2. **Recruit.** Define who qualifies (role, behavior, recent experience). Write a screener of 4 to 6 questions that does not reveal the right answer. Aim for 5 to 8 interviews, or 30 or more survey responses. Find people through the user's customers, communities and a short invite message. Offer a small thank-you.
3. **Interview script (30 minutes).**
   - Intro (2 min): purpose, no right answers, ask permission to take notes or record.
   - Context (5 min): "Tell me about the last time you did X."
   - Story questions (15 min): "What happened next?", "What did you try?", "What was hard?"
   - Wrap (3 min): "Anything I should have asked?", "Who else should I talk to?"
4. **Stay neutral.**
   - Ask about past behavior, not opinions about the future. "Would you use this?" is useless.
   - No leading words: not "Don't you find it frustrating...", but "How did that go?"
   - One question at a time, no yes/no unless you follow up with "why?".
   - Silence is fine. Do not fill it.
5. **Survey tips.** Under 10 questions. Mix one or two open text fields with scaled or multiple choice. Avoid double-barreled items ("fast and easy"). Offer "Other" and "Not applicable".
6. **Synthesize.**
   - Put every observation on its own line in a file, tagged with the participant ID.
   - Group similar lines into themes. Count how many participants support each.
   - Write findings as: theme, evidence (two short quotes), how many of N, and a recommended action.
   - Separate what people said from what you conclude.

Save the plan as `research-plan.md` and results as `findings.md`. Keep names out of the files, use P1, P2.
