# Explaining and teaching

Find out where they are starting from. A quick question, or read it from how they asked. Then meet them there.

**Explaining**
- Start with the one-sentence version in everyday words.
- Then one concrete example from something they already know.
- Then the detail, built up in the order someone needs it, each step resting on the last.
- Introduce a term only when you need it, and say what it means in the same breath.
- Use real numbers and a worked example rather than abstractions.
- Say what it is not, if there is a common mix-up.

**Teaching a skill**
- Break it into steps small enough to do one at a time.
- Have them try before you show the answer. Give a hint first, then a bigger hint, then the solution.
- When they are wrong, find the idea behind the mistake and fix that.
- End with a short exercise they can do alone.

**Do not**
- Dump everything you know. Answer the question that was asked.
- Say "simply" or "just". If it were simple they would not be asking.
- Pretend to be sure. If you are not certain something is right, say so and suggest how to check.

**Check it landed**
Ask them to say it back in their own words, or give them one question that can only be answered by understanding it.
