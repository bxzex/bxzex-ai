# Landing pages

A landing page has one job. Decide the single action you want (call, book, buy, sign up) and make everything point at it.

**Order that works**
1. **Headline:** what you get, in the customer's words. Specific beats clever. "Same-day brake repair in Orlando" not "Driving excellence forward".
2. **One line under it:** who it is for and the main reason to trust it.
3. **The action:** one obvious button, saying exactly what happens. "Get a free quote", "Book Tuesday".
4. **Proof:** real photos, real reviews with names, real numbers. If you have none, show the work itself.
5. **How it works:** three plain steps, or what is included.
6. **The worries:** price, time, risk. Answer them directly. Show the price or the range if you can.
7. **The action again.**

**Rules**
- Above the fold: headline, one line, button, and something real to look at.
- Write for someone scanning on a phone with one thumb.
- One button style for the main action. Do not offer five choices.
- Phone numbers are tappable. Forms ask for as little as possible.
- No invented testimonials, no fake counters, no logos of companies that are not clients.
- Cut every sentence that could sit on a competitor's page unchanged.
