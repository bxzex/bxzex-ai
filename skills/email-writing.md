# Emails and messages

Decide the one thing you want the reader to do. Everything else supports that.

- **Subject:** says what it is and what is needed. "Quote for the kitchen remodel, need a yes by Friday".
- **First line:** the ask or the answer. Not "I hope this finds you well".
- **Then:** only the context they need to say yes. Two or three sentences.
- **Make replying easy:** offer a specific choice ("Tuesday 2pm or Wednesday 10am?") instead of "let me know what works".
- **One email, one topic.** Two asks get one answer.
- **End** with the next step and when. Then stop. No "please don't hesitate to reach out".

Tone
- Write as you would say it across a desk. Polite, direct, no grovelling.
- For bad news: say it in the first two lines, give the reason once, say what happens next.
- For a complaint reply: acknowledge the specific problem, say what you are doing about it and by when. No "we apologize for any inconvenience".
- For a follow-up: add something new. Never just "bumping this".

Length: if it does not fit on a phone screen, cut it or attach the detail.

Give the user the subject line and the body. If the tone is uncertain, offer a warmer and a firmer version.
