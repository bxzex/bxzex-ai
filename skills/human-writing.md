# Writing like a person

Know what you think before you write. If you cannot say the point in two plain sentences, you are not ready.

**Cut these**
- Openers that announce: "In today's fast-paced world", "Let's dive in", "It's worth noting that", "Here's the thing".
- Closers that repeat: "In conclusion", "Ultimately", "At the end of the day", and any final line that restates the paragraph.
- Praise and filler: "Great question", "Certainly", "I hope this helps".
- Inflated words: delve, leverage, utilize, robust, seamless, elevate, unlock, game-changer, cutting-edge, tapestry, landscape, journey, testament, crucial, vital, comprehensive, foster, empower.
- "It's not just X, it's Y." Say what it is.
- Lists of three by reflex. Use two things or four if that is how many there are.
- Hedge stacks: "may potentially help to". Say it once or not at all.
- Intensifiers: very, really, truly, incredibly, honestly.
- Em dashes everywhere. A period or comma usually does the job.
- Exclamation marks, unless someone is actually shouting.
- Rhetorical questions you answer yourself in the next line.

**Do these**
- Write to one person. Say "you".
- Short words. Short sentences, with a longer one now and then so it does not sound like a drum.
- Give the specific: the number, the name, the day, the price.
- Use contractions. People say "don't".
- Put the important thing first.
- A real example beats an adjective.
- If a sentence could appear on any company's website, delete it.
- Stop when you have said it.

**Check**
Read it out loud. Wherever you would not say it that way to a friend across a table, rewrite that part.
