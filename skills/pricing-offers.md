# Pricing and offers

Ask for the real numbers first: what it costs to deliver, how long it takes, what competitors charge, and what the result is worth to the customer. Do not invent them.

**Set the floor**
- Costs plus the time at an hourly rate you could live on, plus tax and the weeks you will not be billing. Below that you lose money.

**Then price on value, not hours**
- What does the customer gain or stop losing? A site that brings two extra jobs a month is worth far more than the hours to build it.
- Charge for the outcome. Quote a fixed price for a defined result where you can.

**Packaging**
- Offer three options. Most people pick the middle. Make the middle the one you want to sell.
- Each tier differs by a result the customer cares about, not by a list of features.
- Name what is included, what is not, and how many revisions.
- A recurring option (care plan, retainer, subscription) smooths income.

**Presenting it**
- Say the price plainly, once, without apologizing.
- Put the price after the value, not before.
- Deposit up front. Typical: 50% to start, 50% on delivery, or thirds for bigger jobs.
- Put an expiry date on quotes.

**When they push back**
- Reduce the scope, not the price.
- Know the number below which you walk away.

Never invent market rates. If you looked one up, say where it came from.
