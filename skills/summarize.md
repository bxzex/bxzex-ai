# Summarizing

Read the whole thing first. A summary of the first third is not a summary.

**Write for a reader who has thirty seconds**
- First sentence: the single most important thing, stated as a fact. Not "This document discusses".
- Then the three to five points that matter, most important first.
- Then anything the reader must do, with dates and names.

**Keep**
- Decisions, numbers, dates, names, deadlines, money.
- Anything surprising or that contradicts what the reader likely assumes.
- Open questions and who owns them.

**Drop**
- Background the reader already knows, pleasantries, repetition, how the discussion got there.

**Stay faithful**
- Do not add opinions or facts that are not in the source.
- Keep the source's level of certainty. "May" stays "may".
- If the source is unclear or contradicts itself, say so instead of smoothing it over.
- Quote exactly when wording matters, such as terms, prices or commitments.

Match the length to the need: a line for a message, a short paragraph for an article, a page at most for a long report. Ask if you are unsure which they want.
